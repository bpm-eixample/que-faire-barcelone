In Catalonia, presents aren't brought by Father Christmas but by the **Three Kings**. And in Barcelona, they arrive by sea. On the evening of **Tuesday 5 January 2027**, Melchior, Caspar and Balthazar land at the foot of the Columbus monument, then ride through the city on their floats in a shower of sweets. It's the best night of the year for children — and a surprising show for visitors.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Date** | Tuesday 5 January 2027 |
| **The Kings arrive** | Around 4:30 pm at Portal de la Pau (foot of the Columbus monument) |
| **Parade** | About 6 pm to 9 pm, from Ciutadella towards Plaça d'Espanya |
| **Price** | Free |
| **Next day** | Wednesday 6 January, public holiday: present day |

## Arrival by sea

Around **4:30 pm**, the Kings' boat docks at **Portal de la Pau**, opposite the Columbus monument at the bottom of La Rambla. The mayor welcomes them and hands over the **magic key** that opens every home in the city for the night. It's the most moving moment for little ones, and the most crowded: arrive at least **an hour early** if you want to see the boat.

## The big parade

At **6 pm**, the parade sets off from Ciutadella Park. In 2026 it lasted just over three hours, with **1,300 performers**, acrobats, brass bands, giant floats for each King and **seven tonnes of (gluten-free) sweets** thrown to the crowd.

The usual route:

| Passing (2026 times) | Where |
|---|---|
| 6:00 pm | Start, Ciutadella Park |
| 6:35 pm | Passeig de Colom / Via Laietana |
| 6:55 pm | Columbus monument |
| 7:30 pm | Avinguda del Paral·lel / Ronda de Sant Pau |
| 7:45 pm | Sant Antoni Market |
| 8:10 – 8:30 pm | Carrer de Sepúlveda |
| 9:10 pm | Finish towards Plaça d'Espanya |

The 2027 route and times are published by the city in late December. With the major redevelopment of the Montjuïc trade-fair grounds — which has already moved the New Year's Eve celebration — **the end of the route may change**: we'll update our [Events](/en/evenements/) page. On the evening itself, the city runs a **live map** showing where the floats are.

## Where to stand with kids

- **Avoid the start and the Columbus monument**: that's where crowds are thickest.
- **Go for Paral·lel or Carrer de Sepúlveda**: wide pavements, and the parade passes later, giving you time to get there.
- **Arrive 45 minutes before** the expected time for a front-row spot.
- **The local families' trick**: an upside-down umbrella or a tote bag to catch the sweets.
- **Closed shoes** and warm clothes: evenings are 8–10 °C and you'll be standing a long time.

## Three Kings traditions

- **The letter to the Kings** — in December, children write their letter and hand it to the **royal pages** (*patges reials*), who tour markets and neighbourhoods. Latecomers can still hand it over on 5 January.
- **Coal** — children who haven't been good get **coal**… made of sugar, black and crunchy, sold in every sweet shop.
- **Shoes on the balcony** — on the night of the 5th, children leave their shoes by the window, with a little water for the camels.
- **The tortell de Reis** — the ring-shaped brioche of 6 January, topped with candied fruit and often filled with marzipan. It hides a **bean** (whoever finds it pays for the cake) and a **figurine** (whoever finds it wears the crown).

## Practical info

- **6 January is a public holiday** (a Wednesday in 2027): most shops are closed. Families open presents in the morning, then have lunch together.
- **The winter sales** traditionally start right after, on **7 January**.
- **The Fira de Reis on Gran Via**, the toy market, stays open until 6 January: perfect for last-minute presents.
- **Neighbourhoods** also hold their own smaller, calmer parades: ideal with toddlers.
- **The metro** is the only way to get around that evening: the whole route is closed to traffic.
