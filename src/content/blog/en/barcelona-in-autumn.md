Ask someone from Barcelona when you should visit their city and they will not say August. Autumn ticks nearly every box — the heat drops, so do the queues and the prices, and the city goes back to its normal rhythm once the summer crowds leave. Here is what September, October, November and December actually feel like.

## The weather, month by month

| Month | Air | Sea | What to expect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **September** | 22–28 °C | 24 °C — perfect swimming | Still summer, a few storms late in the month |
| **October** | 18–24 °C | 22 °C — swimming still fine | The best month: light, terraces, few crowds |
| **November** | 13–19 °C | 18 °C — too cool | A jumper in the evening, a few rainy days |
| **December** | 10–16 °C | — | Dry and bright by day, chilly after dark |

Two things surprise visitors. First, **it doesn't get cold**: December in Barcelona feels like a mild northern-European spring. Second, **autumn downpours are violent but short** — an hour of heavy rain, then the sun comes back. Our [Barcelona in the rain](/en/blog/barcelona-rainy-day/) guide covers the fallback plans.

## What you gain by coming off-season

- **The Sagrada Família without the crush** — morning slots are still available a few days ahead, instead of weeks in summer.
- **Hotel prices 30–40% lower** than in July and August, except during la Mercè and the All Saints' long weekend.
- **Terraces that still work into November**, and restaurants you can walk into on a weekday.
- **A city handed back to its residents**: markets, neighbourhood festivals and bars fill up with locals again.

## September: la Mercè and the end of summer

September is really two months in one. The first fortnight still feels like summer — beach, drinks at 9pm, churros on the sand. Then comes **[la Mercè](/en/blog/la-merce-barcelona/)**, from 23 to 27 September 2026: five days of correfocs, human towers, giants and free concerts that mark the true start of the Barcelona year.

It is also the best month of the year for **swimming**: the sea has stored up the summer heat and stays at 23–24 °C, while the beaches empty out after the 15th.

## October: the perfect month

If you only kept one month, this would be it. The light is superb, it's 23 °C at midday, and the big sights can finally breathe.

- **48h Open House** (24–25 October 2026): over 200 buildings normally closed to the public — modernista flats, air-raid shelters, company headquarters — open free of charge for one weekend. The best free plan of the year.
- **The jazz festival** starts in mid-October and runs to Christmas, from the Palau de la Música to small clubs.
- **La Castanyada**, on 31 October: here, Halloween is mostly celebrated with **roasted chestnuts**, sweet potatoes and **panellets**. The *castanyeres* set up their braziers on the street. 1 November is a public holiday.
- **Hiking** in the Collserola park or up to Montserrat: this is the ideal season, neither too hot nor too humid.

## November: the locals' city

The quietest month of the year, and the cheapest. This is when you do the things that need time: the **MNAC** and the **Miró Foundation** with no queue, a morning at **Sant Antoni market**, a long €13 set lunch, an afternoon of vermouth bars in Gràcia — the ritual described in our [Catalan aperitivo](/en/blog/tapas-aperitivo/) guide makes even more sense at 15 °C.

In late November the city tips into Christmas: **lights** switch on along the main avenues and the **[Fira de Santa Llúcia](/en/evenements/)** opens in front of the cathedral on 26 November.

## December: Christmas, Catalan style

Christmas here has its own rules, and they are genuinely funny.

- The **caganer**: a squatting figurine, trousers down, hidden in the nativity scene. Sold everywhere, often as a celebrity, it symbolises the fertility of the soil.
- The **tió de Nadal**: a log with a painted face that children feed for weeks, then beat with a stick on 24 December so it "produces" presents.
- The **Fira de Santa Llúcia**, 285 stalls in front of the cathedral until 23 December.
- On **5 January**, the **Cavalcada de Reis**: the Three Kings land by sea at Port Vell and parade across the city. For Catalan families this, not 25 December, is the real climax of the holidays.

## Before you book

- **Public holidays** — 12 October, 1 November, 6 and 8 December, 25 and 26 December (St Stephen's Day is a holiday in Catalonia). Museums and shops close or run shorter hours.
- **Spanish long weekends** fill the hotels: All Saints and the 6–8 December bridge are the two peaks of the season.
- **Bring a light waterproof jacket**, not a winter coat. And keep your swimsuit until mid-October.
- **The beach is still worth it** even without swimming: lunch in Barceloneta in November, in full sun, is one of the great pleasures of the season.

## Three autumn days, our default plan

- **Day 1** — Sagrada Família in the morning (9am slot), lunch in the Eixample, afternoon on Passeig de Gràcia and Casa Batlló, vermouth in Sant Antoni.
- **Day 2** — La Boqueria early, the lanes of El Born and Santa Maria del Mar, a nap, sunset at the Bunkers del Carmel, tapas in Gràcia.
- **Day 3** — Montjuïc by cable car, MNAC, lunch in Poble-sec on Carrer de Blai, late afternoon in Barceloneta.
