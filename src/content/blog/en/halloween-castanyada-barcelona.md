In Barcelona, 31 October has two faces. On one side there's **Halloween**, a late arrival that the city has embraced with gusto: costumes, haunted houses and parties until dawn. On the other there's the **Castanyada**, the much older Catalan chestnut festival, celebrated with family around a paper cone of hot chestnuts. In 2026 both fall on a **Saturday night**, followed by **All Saints' Day (Tots Sants) on Sunday 1 November**.

## The Castanyada, Catalonia's real 31 October festival

Long before pumpkins, Catalans marked the eve of All Saints by eating **castanyes** (roasted chestnuts). The custom goes back to old all-night vigils: bells were rung for the dead through the night, and people kept their strength up with cheap, filling autumn food.

### What to eat

- **Chestnuts** — roasted on street braziers by the *castanyeres* and handed over piping hot in a paper cone.
- **Sweet potatoes** (*moniatos*) — roasted, usually sold at the same stalls.
- **Panellets** — the stars of the show: little marzipan balls, most often rolled in pine nuts, but also coconut, coffee or chocolate. Every pastry shop fills its window with them in late October; you buy them by weight.
- **Moscatell** — a glass of sweet dessert wine to wash it all down.

### Where to join in

You don't need a programme: the Castanyada happens in the street. **Chestnut stalls** pop up from mid-October on squares and main avenues, and many neighbourhoods hold chestnut festivals with workshops, storytelling and children's shows — the Sant Martí district (Poblenou) runs events all month. The easiest plan: grab a cone of chestnuts and a box of panellets and enjoy them on a bench on the Rambla del Poblenou or Passeig de Sant Joan.

## Halloween: the outings that (actually) scare

### A haunted Poble Espanyol

The village on Montjuïc turns into **Creepy Halloween Family** on **weekends from 17 October to 1 November 2026**: a trail of mysteries, characters and puzzles through its lanes. Early hours are designed for little ones; the scares ramp up later in the day. On Saturday 31 it closes early, at 7 pm. From **€10.50**, including entry to the whole site (cheaper online).

### Tibidabo

The amusement park high above the city brings out its witches and zombies on its October and early-November opening days: shows, decorations and activities included in the park ticket. Great for families, with the best view of Barcelona thrown in. Check the opening calendar before heading up: in autumn the park isn't open every day.

### PortAventura, for thrill-seekers

About 1 hour 15 minutes from Barcelona by train, **PortAventura World** celebrates Halloween from **19 September to 15 November 2026**, with five haunted houses this year. It's a full day out, but it's the region's big Halloween event.

### Parties on 31 October

On Saturday 31, clubs and bars go all out: costume parties, fancy-dress contests and free guest-list entry at many clubs. Tip: the metro runs **all night from Saturday to Sunday** — use it to get home.

## Tots Sants: All Saints' Day, Catalan style

On 1 November many families lay flowers on their relatives' graves. The city's two great cemeteries are worth a quiet, respectful visit:

- **Montjuïc Cemetery**, on the hillside facing the sea, with its monumental family tombs.
- **Poblenou Cemetery**, the oldest in the city, known for the sculpture *El Petó de la Mort* (the Kiss of Death).

In 2026 All Saints' Day falls on a Sunday: most shops are closed anyway, but museums, restaurants and bars in the centre stay open.

## Practical tips

- **Buy your panellets the day before.** On the evening of the 31st, pastry shops are packed and the best batches sell out.
- **Book Poble Espanyol online** for the weekend of the 31st: it's the busiest, and the web price is lower.
- **Bring a light jacket.** In late October it's still around 20 °C during the day, but evenings get cool.
- **With kids**, do Poble Espanyol early in the afternoon and Tibidabo; your local chestnut festival will take care of the rest.
