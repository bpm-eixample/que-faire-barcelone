Barcelona is Europe's number one city for a **bachelor** or **bachelorette party** — a stag do or hen do, depending on which side of the Atlantic you're on. The reasons are obvious: cheap direct flights from across Europe, a beach inside the city, food that's excellent and affordable, and a nightlife that genuinely runs until sunrise. This guide covers everything you need to decide, in the order you need to decide it.

## The weekend that works

The format hundreds of groups have converged on:

| When | The plan |
|---|---|
| **Friday night** | Arrive, vermouth and tapas in El Born, an easy first bar crawl |
| **Saturday afternoon** | The group activity — the big one |
| **Saturday night** | Tapas dinner → cocktail bars → club |
| **Sunday** | Beach, brunch, fly home |

The golden rule: **one big activity per day**. Overloaded weekends end with an exhausted group that sleeps through Saturday night.

## What it costs

Per person, excluding flights, for two nights:

- **Accommodation**: €60–120 for two nights in a shared apartment; more in a hotel.
- **Group activity**: €25–60 depending on the format (workshop, catamaran, karting…).
- **Food**: €15–25 for a proper tapas dinner, half that for a weekday set lunch.
- **Going out**: €10–14 for a cocktail in a good bar, €15–25 club entry with a drink.

A comfortable stag or hen weekend in Barcelona lands around **€200–300 per person** on the ground. That's meaningfully less than Amsterdam or Dublin for the same quality, which is exactly why the city has become the default.

**The tip that saves the most grief**: set up a group payment pot as soon as dates are fixed, and pay for the accommodation and the activity from it. Chasing twelve bank transfers on Saturday night has ruined more weekends than rain.

## Where to stay: the neighbourhood matters more than the hotel

- **Eixample** — the best balance. Central, quiet at night, well connected, with large apartments that suit groups. Our default recommendation.
- **El Born / Barri Gòtic** — atmosphere and the best cocktail bars on your doorstep, but noisy streets and neighbours with limited patience. Ideal if your group goes to bed late and leaves early.
- **Barceloneta** — the beach below your window. Great in summer, much less practical otherwise, and very touristy.
- **What doesn't work**: booking far from the centre to save €20. You'll spend it on taxis the first night.

One warning: Barcelona regulates tourist rentals strictly. Check that the listing displays a **tourist licence number (HUT)** — without it, last-minute cancellation is a real risk.

## Activities that actually work for groups

The principle is simple: pick something where **everyone does something**, not where everyone watches. Groups remember what they did together.

- **Cocktail or sangria workshop** — the safe bet. You learn something, you drink what you made, everyone takes part, and it starts the night rather than tiring it out. It's the most requested format for stag and hen groups in Barcelona.
- **Sunset catamaran** — two hours at sea with drinks and music. Expensive per hour, but it's the photo of the weekend.
- **Escape room or treasure hunt** in the Gothic Quarter — the best choice when the group doesn't all know each other.
- **Paella or flamenco class** — the cultural option, which wins over the sceptics more often than you'd think.
- **Karting, paintball, beach volleyball** — for groups that need to burn energy.

## Going out

Barcelona drinks in three stages: vermouth, cocktails, club.

**Vermouth (7–9pm)** — the local ritual, on a terrace, with olives and tinned seafood. Our guide to [tapas and the aperitivo](/en/blog/tapas-aperitivo/) explains how to do it properly.

**Cocktails (11pm–1am)** — the city is a world reference here. Paradiso, Sips, Two Schmucks and Dr. Stravinsky sit at the top; **BPM Bar** in Eixample is one of the few places a group of twelve can walk into without overwhelming the bar.

**Club (1–5am)** — Razzmatazz (five rooms, five genres), Sala Apolo, Opium and Pacha on the seafront, La Terrrazza in summer.

## Five mistakes that come up every time

1. **Arriving without a Saturday dinner reservation.** A restaurant that seats twelve people without booking on a Saturday does not exist here.
2. **Assuming twelve of you will get into a club.** Large all-male groups are turned away at the door regularly. Book a table or guest list, or split up.
3. **Forgetting ID.** Checked at every club door, and a photo on your phone won't do.
4. **Stag and hen costumes.** After the city's campaigns against booze tourism, many venues simply refuse costumed groups.
5. **Buying an all-inclusive stag package.** Expensive, standardised, and it sends you to venues that exist only for that trade. Building it yourself costs less and is far better.
