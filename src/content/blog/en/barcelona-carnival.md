In February, Barcelona dresses up. For a week, a grotesque king takes power, neighbourhoods parade in costume, a shower of orange confetti rains down on the crowd… and it all ends with the burial of a sardine. In 2027, Carnival runs **from Thursday 4 to Wednesday 10 February**. And 40 minutes away by train, **Sitges** throws the wildest carnival in Catalonia.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dates** | Thursday 4 to Wednesday 10 February 2027 |
| **Opening** | Dijous Gras (Thursday 4): King Carnestoltes arrives |
| **Parades** | The weekend of 6–7 February, in the neighbourhoods |
| **The end** | Ash Wednesday (10 February): burial of the sardine |
| **The biggest** | Sitges, with its Sunday and Tuesday night parades |
| **Price** | Free |

## Dijous Gras, an opening you eat

Carnival begins on **Fat Thursday**, a day Catalans celebrate first and foremost… at the table. It's the day of **coca de llardons** (a flat pastry with pork crackling and pine nuts), **botifarra d'ou** (egg sausage) and the **truita**: many schools and families head out for a picnic with a Spanish omelette. Every bakery in town fills its window with cocas that day.

## King Carnestoltes and the Taronjada

On the evening of Fat Thursday, **King Carnestoltes**, a pot-bellied prankster, makes his entrance into the city. For a week he "rules" Barcelona and declares the reign of partying. His arrival ends with the **Taronjada**: a huge battle of orange confetti and balloons, a nod to the orange-throwing of old.

Every year a **different district** hosts the King's arrival and the Taronjada (Sants in 2026). The 2027 venue is announced by the city in January — we'll update our [Events](/en/evenements/) page.

## Neighbourhood parades

Over the **weekend** (Saturday 6 and Sunday 7 February in 2027), each neighbourhood holds its own **rua**, a costume parade: schools, local groups, brass bands and home-made floats. It's the locals' carnival, popular and family-friendly. The best-attended are often in **Sants**, **Gràcia** and **Poblenou**. No booking needed: dress up and follow the parade.

## The burial of the sardine

On **Ash Wednesday** (10 February), Carnival dies… in style. A mock funeral procession, complete with fake widows in tears, escorts a **sardine** to its "grave". King Carnestoltes says goodbye, and grilled sardines are often shared. End of the party, start of Lent.

## Sitges, Catalonia's wildest carnival

**40 minutes by train** from Barcelona, the seaside town of Sitges hosts Catalonia's most famous carnival, with a hugely festive atmosphere and a big LGBTQ+ tradition. Two nights not to miss:

- **The Rua de la Disbauxa**, on **Sunday night** (7 February in 2027): around fifty floats, feathers and sequins along the seafront.
- **The Rua de l'Extermini**, on **Tuesday night** (9 February in 2027): the same parade, even wilder, with street parties until morning.

**Getting there**: **R2 Sud** train from Passeig de Gràcia or Sants. On parade nights, trains are packed: leave early and check return times (extra trains usually run on those nights).

## Practical tips

- **Costume is compulsory** — almost. A single accessory is enough to blend in.
- **For neighbourhood parades**, arrive half an hour before the start for a good spot, especially with kids.
- **For Sitges**, book accommodation very early if you want to stay over: the town is full months ahead.
- **Watch out for pickpockets** in the Sitges crowds, as at any big gathering.
- **Carnival isn't a public holiday**: shops, museums and restaurants stay open.
