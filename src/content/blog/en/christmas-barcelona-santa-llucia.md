No snow and rarely colder than 10 °C, but a whole city lit up: Christmas in Barcelona is like nowhere else. Catalan traditions are wonderfully odd — a log that "poops" presents, a little squatting figure hidden in the nativity scene — and in 2026 the city holds the title of **European Capital of Christmas**. Here's how to make the most of it, from late November to the Three Kings.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|

## The Fira de Santa Llúcia, the historic Christmas market

It's the oldest Christmas market in Catalonia, running since **1786**. Every year, hundreds of stalls line **Avinguda de la Catedral**, at the foot of the Gothic cathedral, from late November until **23 December**. Entry is free.

Don't come for mulled wine: the Fira is first and foremost where Catalan families buy everything for their **nativity scene** (*pessebre*). Figurines, moss, cork, little houses, Christmas trees, wreaths… and the two items that made the fair famous:

- **The caganer** — a little figure squatting with his trousers down, hidden in a corner of the nativity scene. A symbol of fertility and good luck, it now comes as footballers, pop stars and politicians. It's THE souvenir to take home.
- **The tió de Nadal** — a log with a smiling face and a red blanket. Children "feed" it for weeks, then on Christmas Eve they hit it with a stick while singing so that it "poops"… sweets and small presents hidden under the blanket.

**Tip**: go on a weekday or in the morning. At weekends after 4 pm, the crowd moves at a crawl.

## Other Christmas markets

- **The Fira de Nadal de la Sagrada Família** — on the basilica square since 1962, smaller and more family-oriented, with the Nativity façade as a backdrop. It usually runs from late November to just before Christmas.
- **The Fira de Reis on Gran Via** — the toy and gift market for the Three Kings, a tradition dating back to 1877. It opens in mid-December and stays until **6 January**.
- **Port Vell** — in recent years, a Christmas funfair with rides and shows has set up on Moll de la Fusta. Check the 2026 programme on our [Events](/en/evenements/) page.

## The Christmas lights

For its year as European Capital of Christmas, Barcelona lights up around **130 km of streets**, from late November to **6 January**. The must-sees: **Passeig de Gràcia**, **Rambla de Catalunya**, **Portal de l'Àngel** and the lanes of the Born. New for 2026: an **illuminated poem** along Passeig de Maragall and the return of **thirteen giant illuminated biscuits** outside the neighbourhood markets.

**Our route**: set off on foot around 6 pm from Plaça de Catalunya, walk up Passeig de Gràcia to the Diagonal, then come back down Rambla de Catalunya. An hour's stroll, free, with the whole city celebrating.

## Traditions worth knowing

- **24 December (Nit de Nadal)** — for many families it's tió night. A **Missa del Gall** (midnight mass) is held at the cathedral.
- **25 December (Nadal)** — family lunch built around **escudella i carn d'olla**: a broth with giant shell-shaped pasta (*galets*), followed by the meats from the stew. Dessert is **turrón** and **neules** (thin rolled wafers) with a glass of cava.
- **26 December (Sant Esteve)** — a public holiday in Catalonia and the day for **canelons**, made with the leftover meat from the day before.
- **Els Pastorets** — a popular nativity play staged in neighbourhood theatres throughout the holidays.
- **The city's nativity scene** — in recent years it has been set up inside the City Hall on Plaça de Sant Jaume, free to visit over the holidays. In the evening, light projections cover the façades of the square.

## Practical tips

- **25 and 26 December are public holidays** (a Friday and a Saturday in 2026): most shops and many restaurants close. On the **evening of the 24th**, many restaurants close early.
- **Book festive meals from November.** For dinner on the 24th or the 31st, good tables go fast. Our tried-and-tested picks are on the [Where to eat](/en/ou-manger/) page.
- **Bring a jacket.** Days are mild (14–15 °C), but evenings are cool and damp.
- **8 December is also a public holiday** (Immaculate Conception, a Tuesday in 2026).

## What about after Christmas?

On **31 December**, locals eat twelve grapes on the twelve strokes of midnight, then the city parties until morning. On **5 January**, the Three Kings land at Port Vell before parading through the city: for Catalan families it's the biggest moment of the season, far more than the 25th.
