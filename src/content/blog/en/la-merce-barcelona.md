Every September, Barcelona stops working for five days. **La Mercè**, the city's main festival, turns streets, squares and beaches into one enormous stage — and almost all of it is **free**. In 2026 it runs from **Wednesday 23 to Sunday 27 September**.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dates** | 23–27 September 2026 |
| **Where** | Everywhere: Ciutat Vella, Montjuïc, the seafront, all ten districts |
| **Price** | Free for almost everything, BAM and Música Mercè concerts included |
| **Peak day** | 24 September, the official feast day and a local public holiday |
| **2026 guest city** | Shanghai |

## Where the festival comes from

La Mercè honours the **Virgin of Mercy**, co-patron of Barcelona since 1687, the year the city credited her with ending a plague of locusts. The modern festival dates from 1902: a free, popular, secular programme that today runs to more than **500 activities** and draws around two million people.

## Five things not to miss

### 1. The correfoc

This is the image everyone takes home. "Devils" carrying firework-tipped pitchforks run through the crowd to the beat of drums, in a shower of sparks. It isn't a show you watch from a distance — regulars **step into the fire**, wearing thick cotton clothes, a hood, a cap and closed shoes. Nothing synthetic, never shorts. A gentler children's correfoc (*correfoc infantil*) takes place earlier in the day.

### 2. The castells

The **human towers**, listed by UNESCO as intangible heritage, are built on Plaça Sant Jaume. Dozens of people stack up eight or nine levels high, until a child climbs to the top and raises a hand. The silence of the crowd during the climb is worth the trip on its own. Get there **45 minutes early** if you want to see anything.

### 3. Giants and parades

The *gegants*, towering figures carried on people's shoulders, parade with the *capgrossos* (big heads) and the *bestiari* — dragons, the mulassa, the city eagle. The most impressive format is the **cercavila**, the procession that winds through the Gothic Quarter.

### 4. BAM and Música Mercè

Two **free** open-air music festivals in several parts of the city. **BAM** (Barcelona Acció Musical) programmes the independent and emerging scene; **Música Mercè** is more mainstream, mixing pop, Catalan rumba and world music. Full line-ups come out in early September.

### 5. The piromusical

The grand finale: a **fireworks, music and water** show on the Sunday night that closes the festival. It traditionally takes place at the Font Màgica on Montjuïc, but because of works on the hill **the 2026 edition is launched from Bogatell beach**, on **Sunday 27 September at 10pm**. The good news: it can be seen from much of the seafront, from Barceloneta to Poblenou.

## MAC, the street-arts side of la Mercè

**MAC (Mercè Arts de Carrer)** fills parks and squares with circus, dance and street theatre: Parc de la Ciutadella, Fabra i Coats, Moll de la Fusta. It is the most family-friendly part of the programme, runs in daylight, and is often the most surprising — international companies performing for free in front of a few hundred people.

## Practical advice

- **24 September is a public holiday in Barcelona.** Many shops close or open reduced hours, while museums often go free that weekend. Check before you set off.
- **Take the metro.** Whole streets are closed, buses rerouted, and taxis take forever. The metro runs all night from Saturday to Sunday.
- **Book restaurants.** Over these five days good tables in the centre fill up days in advance. Our tested addresses are on the [Where to eat](/en/ou-manger/) page.
- **Have a backup plan for the evening.** After a correfoc or a concert, central bars overflow. Our picks by neighbourhood are on the [Nightlife](/en/vie-nocturne/) page.
- **Watch for pickpockets.** This is the year's peak for crowded streets: bag in front, no phone in a back pocket.
- **Accommodation doubles in price** that week. If you're reading this for 2027, book in spring.

## What to see, depending on who you are

- **With kids** — MAC during the day at the Ciutadella, the children's correfoc, the giants. Skip the evening correfoc.
- **For music** — BAM, after dark, across several open-air stages.
- **For tradition** — Plaça Sant Jaume: castells on Sunday morning, the *toc d'inici* and sardanes.
- **For photos** — Sunday's piromusical, and the correfoc devils at the start of the route before the crowd closes in.

## And after la Mercè?

September is when Barcelona's real season starts: fewer people than in August, sea still at 23 °C, terraces open until midnight.
