Planning a **bachelor or bachelorette party** in Barcelona isn't hard; it's simply badly distributed in time. Organisers are late on accommodation and far too early on details. Here's the method, in order.

## First: the three questions

Before booking anything, settle three points with the person getting married — or with those closest to them, if it's a surprise.

1. **What's off the table?** Explicitly. This one conversation prevents 90% of weekends that go wrong.
2. **What's the per-person ceiling?** A number, not a range. It determines everything else.
3. **Who's coming?** The final list drives the accommodation, and accommodation is booked first.

## The countdown

**3 months out** — Fix the dates and close the guest list. Book flights: this is where most of the total budget is decided.

**2 months out** — Book accommodation. Large, well-located apartments go early in Barcelona, especially May to September. Check the listing shows a **tourist licence number (HUT)**: undeclared flats get pulled from platforms, sometimes days before arrival.

**6 weeks out** — Book the main Saturday activity. Saturday afternoon slots fill first, particularly in high season.

**1 month out** — Book Saturday dinner. A restaurant that seats twelve without a booking on a Saturday does not exist.

**2 weeks out** — Club table or guest list if the group is large. Create the group chat, send the plan, remind everyone about ID.

**The day before** — One message with addresses, times and the organiser's number. Nobody reads a twelve-page PDF.

## The budget, line by line

Two nights, excluding flights, per person:

| Item | Range |
|---|---|
| Accommodation (2 nights) | €60 – 120 |
| Main activity | €25 – 60 |
| Meals (3–4) | €60 – 90 |
| Going out (bars + club) | €50 – 90 |
| Local transport | €15 – 25 |
| **Total** | **€210 – 385** |

Always add **10% headroom**. There's always an unplanned taxi, a round someone buys, or a door charge higher than advertised.

**Set up the pot immediately.** Open a group payment pot as soon as dates are agreed, and pay for accommodation and the activity from it. The organiser who fronts €1,500 and chases transfers for two months is a cliché — and it poisons the mood.

## The shape of the weekend

Two nights is the right length: Friday warms up, Saturday carries the weekend, Sunday ends at the beach or over brunch. Three nights tire the group and inflate the budget without adding anything.

One simple principle: **one structuring activity per day, not three**. Keep the rest loose. The best moments of a stag or hen weekend are rarely the ones on the schedule.

## Where to stay, in one line

**Eixample** for comfort and quiet, **El Born** for atmosphere and bars on your doorstep, **Barceloneta** if it's a summer, beach-focused weekend. The detail is in our guide to [Barcelona's neighbourhoods](/en/blog/barcelona-neighbourhoods/).

## The arguments that always come up

**Surprise or not?** A surprise destination works. A completely surprise programme doesn't: the person it's for needs to be able to rule things out.

**Big group or small?** Past fifteen people everything becomes logistics — restaurants, club doors, taxis. Between eight and twelve, everything is easy.

**Restaurant or tapas?** Tapas, almost always: they circulate, they're shared, and they don't pin the group to one table for two hours. Our picks are on the [where to eat](/en/ou-manger/) page.

## The departure checklist

- Original ID — checked at every club door
- European Health Insurance Card, or equivalent travel cover
- The accommodation address written down (there's no signal in the metro)
- A shared payment method for group bills
- The organiser's number saved by everyone
